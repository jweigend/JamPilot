Ever wanted to just grab your instrument and play along with whatever is coming out of your computer? The live take nobody ever wrote a chord sheet for, the B-side no tutorial will ever cover. Not “look it up, learn it, come back next week”. Now.

No more googling for chords. Press play, and play.

The delay is the feature

JamPilot taps your system audio, holds it back for a few seconds, and plays it to your speakers delayed but otherwise untouched. What it analyses is the fresh signal — the part you have not heard yet.

So the chord is on your screen before it reaches your ears. Five seconds of delay is the default: about two seconds of lead on the lane, long enough to see a change coming, short enough that you still feel like you are playing with the record.

You do not notice the delay while playing: what you hear and what you see are in sync, alone or with the whole room on the same speakers.

The big chord

What is sounding right now, in sync with your speakers — not what the analysis is chewing on.

The lane

The future, moving towards the NOW line. A chord flips to the centre in exactly the frame its chip touches the line.

Bar lines

A faint tick per beat, the downbeat a little taller. The chip says what comes, the ticks say where in the bar.

The waveform underneath

The music’s own attacks under the chips. Where a chord is held for bars, the peaks show you where the bass hits — before you hear them.

Key and scale degrees

JamPilot works out the key, spells every chord to match (B♭, not A♯) and writes Nashville numbers above the names. Know the key? Pin it.

Record mode

Missed a change? Press R and step back and forth chord by chord, like a CD player — then return to live.

Your instrument, not just a chord name

Every device picks its own view — your phone and your laptop may disagree.

Chords. The audible chord, big, with its scale degree above. F is about to arrive, C follows in 0.8 seconds — enough time to get your hand there.

Bass. The chord says what the band plays; it does not say what the bass plays. JamPilot measures the bass note (C/E) and shows it on a four-string neck — in the octave the bassist is actually in, with the next note already marked.

Guitar. A fretboard diagram for the sounding chord. The shape is chosen with the lead: a look-ahead over the coming chords picks the path with the least hand travel, so you stay in one position instead of jumping across the neck.

Keyboard. The chord as pressed keys on two octaves, in the inversion that keeps your right hand in place — with the measured bass marked for the left.

One computer listens. The whole band reads.

Scan the QR code with any phone or tablet on the same Wi-Fi and the same synced display is on every music stand in the room — each set to its own instrument.

No app, no account: it is just a browser page. The computer does the listening; every other device is a screen.

Two things worth knowing before you start

Because you would find them out anyway.

You hear the song a few seconds late

That is not a glitch, it is the deal: those seconds are what gets the chord onto your screen before your ears get the music.

Harmony, not transcription

JamPilot names the harmony — triads, sevenths, sus, dim, aug, 6 — and shows where to play it. It does not transcribe riffs, melodies or solos note by note, and it folds jazz extensions into their core chord. Pop, rock, blues and folk are its home turf; recognition is very good, not perfect.

Download

Nothing to install. The latest release, straight from GitHub.

Windows

x86_64 · verified on Windows 10

Download for Windows
  1. Unzip, then double-click JamPilot.cmd.
  2. SmartScreen asks once: More info → Run anyway (the program is not code signed).
  3. Allow the firewall for private networks, or the QR code will not work.

Linux

x86_64 · the reference platform

Download for Linux
  1. Unpack the archive.
  2. Once, from a terminal: ./jampilot install
  3. From then on it is in your app menu.

macOS

Apple Silicon · audio setup by hand

Download for macOS
  1. Unpack, double-click JamPilot.app.
  2. macOS blocks unsigned apps: System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway.
  3. Not one-click yet: it needs BlackHole and two flags — the steps.

The very first start takes up to a minute — it unpacks and builds its caches. Nothing has crashed; every start after that takes seconds.

A personal note

Why is JamPilot free?

By now there are about three months of work in JamPilot, plus a little infrastructure and the bill for Claude Code. So why give it away?

The first reason: I want to give something back. From 2000 to 2020 I wrote software for a living, and it paid really well. Today I do not depend on this project earning its costs back.

But there is a point behind that which matters more to me. My colleagues and I used a great deal of open source software in our commercial products: about ten percent of the code was our own, the large remainder came from others. Those components were, as a rule, superbly built and tested — the Apache Software Foundation did wonderful work, and so did Linux and GNU. Without open source software I could never have done my job the way I did. JamPilot is my way of giving some of that back to the community.

The second reason: JamPilot takes nobody’s business away. It does not compete with commercial tools like Moises or Chordify, nor with professional DAWs. Many of the tools I know can detect, display and analyse chords. But most of the time you first have to upload or import something, and that is exactly what makes them awkward for playing along.

JamPilot does exactly one thing: it holds your computer’s sound back for a few seconds and shows you the chord before you hear it. That is why it needs a computer — on Android and iOS, an app cannot hold back another app’s sound. That is what JamPilot is built for, no more and no less: play‑along. Karaoke for musicians.

And it stays free. A pro version would compete with exactly the tools that already exist. If I ever build something like that, it will be an entirely new tool.

I am one hundred percent convinced of what it is worth, and I am handing JamPilot to the community with my whole heart. If it enables or encourages people to pick up an instrument, it has done what I set out to do.

A tool built with AI that, in the end, helps us become more human again: not just consuming music, but making it ourselves.

Thank you for your interest.

Joao

November 2026

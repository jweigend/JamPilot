Your instrument

The chord says what the band plays. It does not say what a bass player plays: in C/E the chord is C, and the bass is on E. JamPilot measures the bass separately – it is not guessed from the chord.

Fretboard

Bass, guitar and keyboard mode show a diagram top-left. If you know your way around your instrument, turn it off – the big display gets the full screen back.

Control guitar

A quiet, dry guitar pluck at every detected chord change. It is deliberately simple: wrong roots and major/minor thirds become audible immediately without turning JamPilot into an accompaniment.

Key

Spelling and scale degrees hang on the key. Detection needs a few seconds of music and can mishear ambiguous songs – if you know the key, pin it: degrees and spelling are reliable instantly. The pin stays until you switch back.

Chord spelling

The same key is called A♯ or B♭, depending on the key of the song. JamPilot detects the key and spells accordingly – or you decide.

Scale degrees

Nashville-style numbers with the chords – on the timeline and over the big chord: 1 is the key's root, 5 its fifth, and ♭ marks a root outside the key's major scale. A progression looks the same in every key – degrees appear once the key is settled.
